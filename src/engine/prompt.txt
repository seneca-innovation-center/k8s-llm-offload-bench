The history of computing is a story of layered abstractions, each built on the one below it. Engineers first wired logic gates by hand, then wrote machine code, then assemblers and compilers. Operating systems arrived to share scarce hardware between many programs and users at once. Networks connected those machines, and soon the network itself became the computer. Today large neural networks are served from clusters where memory, not arithmetic, is often the limit. Every token a language model produces must pass through all of its layers, one after another.
